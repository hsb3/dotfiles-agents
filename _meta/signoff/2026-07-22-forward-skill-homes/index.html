Why "which home" is a real question, not a formality

You own three related products and these skills could live in different ones:

• dotfiles-agents — the Claude Code marketplace this repo publishes. Ships bundles like exec-desk, code-desk, and standalone skills.
• desk-standard — the separate "executive-desk governor" repo. It's the thing that actually defines how an exec desk works, and it's heading to its own 1.0. You've deliberately not started distributing it through dotfiles-agents yet, to avoid churn in both repos while it's under heavy development.

The tension: skills that drive an exec-desk workflow (like the decision-loop) arguably belong with desk-standard (the governor), not as an exec-desk bundle inside dotfiles-agents. If you build them here now and later move the exec-desk surface to desk-standard, you'd have to move or re-home them — rework. That's the "estate-review gate": the bigger review of how the three products fit together (still awaiting your IA approval) could change where the exec-desk lives.

This gate bites the three skills unequally, which is why they're separate questions below rather than one yes/no.

#137 · decision-loop

decision-loop — proposed home: the exec-desk bundle (here)

What it is: a skill that drives the executive desk's decision workflow — draft a proposed decision, package a ruling batch for you, capture the ruling and flip it to accepted, handle supersession/corrections and decision-number collisions.

Implication of building it here now: this is the skill most exposed to the desk-standard question. Decision-workflow mechanics are core "governor" behaviour. If the estate review moves the exec-desk surface to desk-standard, a decision-loop authored here as an exec-desk bundle would likely have to be re-homed. Highest rework risk.

Recommendation: hold — wait for the estate/IA review, because this one most plausibly belongs to desk-standard rather than to a dotfiles-agents bundle.

release-loop — proposed home: the code-desk bundle (here)

"Code desk" is squarely a dotfiles-agents concept and release mechanics are repo-facing, not governor behaviour — so it's a more natural fit here than decision-loop. It's mainly coupled to #137 by wanting a consistent "do we lock desk homes now or after the review" answer. It could even prove itself on this repo's own publish flow.

Recommendation: hold — low risk on its own, but grouping it with #137 keeps the desk-boundary decision consistent; flip to approve if you'd rather unblock it independently.
