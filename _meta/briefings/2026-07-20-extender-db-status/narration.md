# Extender-DB status — audio narration script (2026-07-20)

This is a status update on the extender database project, recorded July twentieth, twenty twenty-six. The headline: the coverage milestone, M1, is complete. Every extender in the marketplace is now mapped to the jobs it serves, and the database can finally answer its core question — what does the estate cover, and where are the holes — with cited evidence instead of impressions.

First, a quick refresher on what this project is. Extender-db is a PocketBase database of the thirty-seven agent extenders in the dotfiles-agents marketplace — twenty-three authored skills, four agents, four hooks, and six externals — joined to the mental models used to judge and compose them. The design bet is doctrine as data: frameworks like the skill archetypes, the authoring specs, and the jobs-to-be-done taxonomy live as database rows with provenance, and assessments join them to the catalog. The repo stays the source of truth; the database is a projection that can be rebuilt at any time.

Here's where things stand. The database now holds one thousand five hundred fifty assessments, and every single one links back to the exact prompt and verbatim response that produced it. Today's milestone added the coverage layer: of the twenty-four jobs the estate needs done, nineteen are covered, three are only partially served, and two are open gaps — research-question and produce-dataviz. And here's the punchline of the coverage work: nothing that's missing needs to be authored from scratch. Every gap and weak spot already has a candidate in the estate — the deep-research, dataviz, and update-config skills sitting at the user level, a migration playbook that belongs inside foreman-kit, and Anthropic's claude-in-chrome for browser work.

A word on how this was verified, because the method matters as much as the numbers. Seven inexpensive judge agents fanned out over the catalog and emitted structured verdicts with evidence quotes — they never touched the database. Two senior reviewers then blind re-derived a sample without seeing the judges' answers, and every disagreement was adjudicated against the actual skill bodies. Ten verdicts changed. The interesting part: blind review didn't just correct errors, it found coverage the judges missed — a screenshot-capture capability that turned one hard gap into partial coverage. The disagreements also produced four reusable judging rules that feed directly into the next milestone's evaluation rubric.

One redundancy was confirmed and survived adversarial checking: the vendored excalidraw skill from coleam00 duplicates the authored one, and the recommendation is to drop it. Two other duplication claims were refuted — they turned out to be composition, a base skill under a theme layer, and a worker under an orchestrator.

The road ahead runs on two tracks. Track one is our own intellectual property: an analysis surface, then the combine-and-coalesce work that consumes today's findings. Track two is evaluation, and it's deliberately adoptive: the methodologies come from SkillOpt and ClosedLoop, and execution reuses your existing meta-harness on cheap models. Nothing bespoke gets built.

What's needed from you is queued as issue one fifty-three, six decision groups, each with a stated default so a single thumbs-up accepts them all: the five roster promotions, dropping the duplicative excalidraw skill, accepting vendor coverage for improve-code, approving the obsidian-c-l-i fix that satisfies both remaining promotion-gate criteria at once — and the one hard blocker, the pointer to your meta-harness, without which the evaluation track can't start.

Bottom line: the foundation is complete and verified, the operating procedures are written down for future sessions, and the project is one decision batch away from starting its evaluation phase.
